BinaryCeramics

Forms shaped by algorithm, made real in clay.

Shop the collections ↗ Commissions

AI motion study from a studio photograph

BinaryCeramics started as research and became a studio. I wanted to understand how digital systems and physical materials could coexist in a meaningful way, and clay turned out to be the ideal opponent for a machine.

Each collection begins with a rule, a constraint or a dataset: a single continuous line, the rotation of a storm, the amplitude of a song. I don't design a final form. I design a system that produces forms, and each ceramic object is a snapshot of that system at one moment.

The name holds the same duality: the zero and one of code, and the balance the work tries to keep between digital and physical, machine precision and material plasticity, system and intuition.

Process

rule → toolpath → machine → kiln

    Rule

    A form begins as a constraint: one line, one rotation, one dataset. Computational design lets complex patterns emerge from very few rules.

    Toolpath

    The system is written as a single continuous path for the nozzle, so the surface texture lives in the movement itself.

  3. {% include img.html key="bc/delta-printer" alt="Large delta clay printer in the Athens studio" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 25vw, 100vw" %}

    Machine

    I design and build the printers and extruders, from a large-format delta printer to aluminium ram extruders turned on the lathe.

    Kiln

    Drying, firing and glazing. This is where gravity, heat and chance finish what the code started.
